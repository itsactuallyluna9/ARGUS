The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) updated its global advisory for polio last week, adding two new countries to the health notice.

The advisory, which was listed under the agency's second-lowest Level 2, includes warnings for more than two dozen countries around the globe, including popular favorites like the United Kingdom and Germany. The most recent update added Laos and Namibia to the list and removed Finland, Ghana, Spain, and Zimbabwe. 

"The list of countries in the [Travel Health Notice] is informed by the latest global epidemiological data and used to make recommendations regarding required measures to support polio eradication," the CDC wrote. "This list is comprised of countries that have detected poliovirus in humans or environmental samples in the past 13 months."

The CDC recommended all travelers be up to date on their vaccinations. In addition, the agency said adult travelers may consider getting an inactivated polio vaccine booster if they have completed their routine polio vaccine series and have not yet received an adult booster dose.

In general, the CDC recommended polio vaccines for children and said adults who have not yet been vaccinated should receive three doses: the first dose at any time, the second dose one to two months later, and the third dose six to 12 months later.

A "highly contagious infection," polio may not affect everyone who becomes infected, but can have serious consequences for some who do, according to the Harvard Medical School. For some, "the virus attacks nerve cells in the brain and spinal cord, particularly the nerve cells in the spinal cord that control muscles involved in voluntary movement such as walking."

Symptoms include fever, tiredness, nausea, headache, nasal congestion, sore throat, cough, stiffness in the neck and back, and pain in the arms and legs, according to the CDC, along with paralysis in rare cases.

"No drug can kill the virus once an infection has begun," Harvard noted. "Treatment is directed at controlling the symptoms of the disease."

The CDC issues advisories from the lowest Level 1 to the highest Level 4, complete with a warning to "avoid all travel." Under Level 2, the CDC encourages travelers to "practice enhanced precautions."

Beyond the polio warning, the CDC has issued travel advisories for rabies in India, for the mosquito-borne chikungunya virus, for measles around the world, and more. Currently, there are no active CDC travel notifications listed under Level 3 or Level 4.
